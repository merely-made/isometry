## What it is

Paredros is a second-person action RPG in a persistent generated world. You
name one creature and ordinarily inhabit that life until it dies. Around you
are other named creatures with lives of their own, encountered in settlements,
dungeons, ruins, underground regions, and the open world. You may help,
persuade, equip, house, work beside, coordinate with, avoid, rob, or fight
them. Allies are useful, not required, and never units in a party.

Control changes only through an explicit world event or an optional player
rule. Death is the ordinary transition. Creative or accessibility settings
may allow tag-in; difficult or obscure world mechanics may permit possession,
domination, transplantation, cloning, resurrection, or stranger continuities.
The world retains the body and the consequences left behind.

A paredros is the one who sits beside you — in the Greek Magical Papyri an
acquired companion, and in classical civic use an assessor seated beside a
magistrate. A colleague, not a servant. Colleagues also succeed to office.

## Pillars

1. **I live with you.** The relationship is second person: peers you address,
   never units you command. A change of viewpoint does not erase the
   relationship between particular people.
2. **One life makes it real.** You ordinarily remain one named creature until
   death. Body-changing exceptions are world mechanics with costs and
   consequences, not a character-select menu.
3. **The world does not orbit control.** Named creatures, settlements, ruins,
   structures, histories, and cultures continue whether or not the player has
   recruited, visited, or even discovered them.
4. **Legible or it isn't real.** Depth nobody notices is procedural noise, so
   the surface that shows why two characters are close ships with the
   simulation, not after it.
5. **Sincere, not edgy.** Affectionate and mortal, in RimWorld's vanilla
   register.
6. **Playable alone.** RNG worlds always work. An inherited world displaces
   the procedural one; it never gates it.
7. **Culture has causes.** Choices become remembered deeds; repeated practices
   become expectations, norms, roles, buildings, rituals, and institutions.
   An ideology is a legible history, not a generated modifier card.

## Where it sits

Vessel 2 of a three-game wing that shares a world substrate, a lineage model,
and a trust plane — engine organs are shared where they stay verb-neutral —
but no genre, schedule, or verbs: Mesocosm (first person), Paredros (second
person), Isometry (third person).

See `mesocosm/design_docs/2026-07-30_games_wing_founding.md`.

## Speculative

Not planned, recorded only as direction: co-op with shared burdens rather
than shared targets; companions who travel to other players' settlements as
admitted denizens; settlements exporting as playable Isometry campaigns where
the dead appear as named figures.
